# Quick Prompt — College Canteen Optimization

## Challenge
You are an expert in **food-service operations, student behavior, pricing strategy, inventory management, and demand forecasting**.

A college canteen has a budget of **₹10,000 for one week** to improve its performance. The goal is to decide **what food to offer, how much to charge, how to manage inventory and demand, and how to use the budget efficiently**.

Create a practical, data-driven one-week plan that maximizes student satisfaction and operational efficiency while staying within the ₹10,000 budget.

## Your Task

Develop a complete canteen strategy covering:

1. **Menu selection**
   - Recommend the food and beverage items to sell.
   - Prioritize items based on likely student demand, affordability, preparation time, shelf life, and profit potential.
   - Include a mix of filling meals, snacks, and beverages.

2. **Pricing**
   - Give a selling price for every recommended item.
   - Keep prices realistic for college students.
   - Explain the basic reasoning behind the pricing.

3. **₹10,000 budget allocation**
   - Divide the ₹10,000 across ingredients, beverages, packaging, contingency/wastage, and any other necessary costs.
   - Show the allocation in a clear table.
   - Do not exceed ₹10,000.

4. **Demand and inventory management**
   - Estimate daily demand for each major item.
   - Explain how much stock should be prepared initially and when to restock.
   - Minimize food waste while avoiding stockouts.
   - Account for different demand levels on weekdays versus weekends or high-footfall days.

5. **Profitability**
   - Estimate cost price, selling price, expected units sold, revenue, and approximate profit for each major item.
   - Calculate the estimated total weekly revenue and profit.
   - Clearly state assumptions where exact data is unavailable.

6. **Student experience**
   - Suggest ways to make the canteen more attractive without significantly increasing costs.
   - Consider combo meals, student-friendly deals, peak-hour service, digital ordering, and feedback collection.

7. **Risk management**
   - Identify the biggest risks: unsold food, ingredient price changes, unexpected demand, shortages, and low-selling items.
   - Give a simple action plan for each risk.

8. **Optimization**
   - Identify which items should be:
     - **High-stock / high-priority**
     - **Medium-stock**
     - **Made-to-order / low-stock**
   - Explain why.

## Output Format

Present the final answer in this order:

### 1. Executive Summary
A concise overview of the strategy.

### 2. Recommended Menu
| Item | Estimated Cost | Selling Price | Expected Daily Sales | Expected Margin | Stock Priority |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

### 3. ₹10,000 Budget Plan
| Category | Amount | % of Budget | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|

### 4. Weekly Demand & Inventory Plan
Provide a practical day-by-day plan from Day 1 to Day 7.

### 5. Revenue & Profit Projection
Show the calculations and clearly distinguish **revenue, costs, gross profit, and leftover inventory/wastage assumptions**.

### 6. Student Deals & Experience
Give 3–5 low-cost ideas that can increase student satisfaction and sales.

### 7. Risk & Contingency Plan
Show the main risks and the specific action to take if each occurs.

### 8. Final Strategy
End with the **5 most important actions** the canteen manager should implement immediately.

## Important Constraints

- The total initial budget must be **≤ ₹10,000**.
- Assume an Indian college environment and prices in **INR (₹)**.
- Keep the plan realistic for a **one-week experiment**, not a theoretical business plan.
- Prioritize foods that can realistically be prepared and served in a college canteen.
- Avoid assuming unlimited kitchen capacity, staff, refrigeration, or storage.
- Explicitly state assumptions about student footfall and demand.
- Do not invent precise market data; use reasonable estimates and label them as assumptions.
- Optimize for a balance of **affordability + student satisfaction + low wastage + profitability**.
- Use calculations that are internally consistent.
- If a recommendation depends heavily on an unknown variable such as student footfall, show how the plan should change under **low-, medium-, and high-demand scenarios**.

**Think like a canteen operator, financial planner, and student at the same time. The final plan should be practical enough that a college canteen manager could implement it tomorrow.**
